# Evidence Record

The Belmont principles of respect for persons, beneficence, and justice lead to
requirements for informed and voluntary consent, systematic assessment of the
probability and magnitude of harms and benefits, and equitable participant
selection. Relevant harms include psychological, social, legal, and economic
harms, not only physical injury.

The handoff study therefore cannot reuse employee work traces or recruit
subordinates merely because they are convenient. Participation, withdrawal,
identity protection, and non-use for performance management must be explicit.

## Limitations

Belmont is ethical guidance, not a repository-specific legal determination.
Applicability of the Common Rule or other law requires qualified review for the
actual sponsor, participants, location, funding, and intended dissemination.
