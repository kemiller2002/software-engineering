# Technical Communication Analysis

## Foundational model

Minimalism treats documentation as support for users' situated tasks and
reasoning, favoring action-oriented, selective guidance over exhaustive system
description [EV-TECHCOMM-2026-14B2]. Audience, purpose, genre, information
quality, structured authoring, and content maintenance are established concerns.

## Recent and empirical evidence

Industrial application remains difficult because “minimal” is contextual and
the principles are abstract [EV-TECHCOMM-2026-6FD1]. DORA makes clarity,
findability, reliability, accuracy, currency, and goal support measurable
quality dimensions [EV-HANDOFF-2026-4F92].

## Competing interpretations and counterevidence

More detail can help safety, novice learning, and rare recovery tasks.
Minimal guidance can omit rationale and boundary conditions. Document quality
may proxy healthy organization rather than cause outcomes. A handoff is also a
commitment and coordination device, not only guidance.

## Reusable methods and outcomes

Task and audience analysis; usability and action tests; findability; error
recovery; time-on-task; maintenance/decay; authoring cost; confidence calibration.

## Coverage and gap

This body explains recipient/task orientation, selective disclosure, structured
content, and documentation quality. It does not alone explain authority,
cross-team commitments, system risk, or whether an artifact remains valid.

## Assumptions challenged

“Complete is better” and “shorter is better” are both rejected. Relevance and
action are contextual. A template is not a method without behavioral evidence.

## Discriminating experiment

Compare a minimalist handoff with the proposed integrated profile. If the
profile adds no correct-continuation, risk-detection, or lifecycle-cost benefit,
technical communication already explains the artifact-design problem.

## Confidence and limits

High confidence in overlap; Medium transfer confidence. Targeted, not systematic.

## Ten future questions

1. Which information can be omitted without increasing unsafe assumptions?
2. Does task orientation postpone conceptual learning?
3. When does rationale outperform procedure?
4. How should minimalism change with failure severity?
5. Which quality dimension predicts correct continuation?
6. Does modular reuse increase stale-context errors?
7. Can calibration improve without substantial length?
8. What authoring signals predict maintenance cost?
9. Do novice and expert handoffs require different genres?
10. Does integration outperform minimalism after controlling author effort?
