# CSCW and Coordination Analysis

## Foundational model

CSCW treats collaboration as accomplishment through people, artifacts,
practices, awareness, division of labor, and articulation work. Boundary
objects support coordination without identical interpretations; common ground
is produced, not assumed.

## Recent and industrial evidence

Research with 53 automotive practitioners distinguishes local from
cross-boundary artifacts [EV-CSCW-2026-32A9]. A longitudinal industrial study
organizes effective boundary artifacts around scope, structure, and management,
while finding trust and commitments can cause failure [EV-CSCW-2026-9C40].

## Competing interpretations and counterevidence

Shared artifacts can coordinate without consensus. Standardization can erase
useful local interpretation. Accurate artifacts fail when trust, authority,
ownership, or commitment fail. Fewer questions may signal disengagement.

## Reusable methods and outcomes

Workplace observation; interaction and artifact-ecology analysis; coordination
delay; rework; network analysis; trust/commitment assessment; breakdown analysis.

## Coverage and gap

CSCW already explains handoffs as coordination arrangements and boundary
objects. It does not itself prescribe software assurance, claim provenance, or
risk-weighted validation.

## Assumptions challenged

Failure is not mainly missing content; consensus is not always required; an
accurate artifact cannot compensate for missing authority or trust.

## Discriminating experiment

Compare artifact-only improvement with an intervention adding ownership,
response expectations, escalation, and feedback. Dominance of the latter would
identify coordination as the primary mechanism.

## Confidence and limits

High confidence CSCW owns the mechanism; Medium handoff-transfer confidence.

## Ten future questions

1. Which outcomes need common ground versus compatible action?
2. When does interpretive flexibility improve coordination?
3. How do power and authority affect apparent success?
4. Which management practice prevents trust decay?
5. Can boundary objects work under conflicting incentives?
6. Does ownership outperform additional content?
7. When is clarification a positive coordination act?
8. Which breakdowns are detectable from artifact history?
9. When does standardization destroy local usefulness?
10. What value remains beyond CSCW plus software assurance?
