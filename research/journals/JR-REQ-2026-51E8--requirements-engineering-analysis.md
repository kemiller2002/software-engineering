# Requirements Engineering Analysis

## Foundational model

Requirements engineering treats communication as iterative elicitation,
negotiation, specification, validation, traceability, rationale, and controlled
evolution. Ambiguity is partly inevitable; the target is managed uncertainty,
not merely unambiguous prose.

## Recent and industrial evidence

Only 28 of 174 mapped ambiguity papers had empirical evaluation and most focused
on detection rather than reduction [EV-REQ-2026-48A0]. A recent automotive
dataset exposes trace links, decisions, deviation rationales, and domain
references across requirement transformation [EV-REQ-2026-D781].

## Competing interpretations and counterevidence

Formal precision can exclude users; traceability can become compliance theater;
ambiguity can enable negotiation; requirements can become wrong as context
changes.

## Reusable methods and outcomes

Ambiguity taxonomies; stakeholder validation; trace coverage; rationale capture;
change-impact analysis; defect leakage; volatility; decision latency.

## Coverage and gap

RE covers action-bearing artifacts, rationale, traceability, validation, and
evolution. Its common unit is intended system behavior, not transient work
state, and empirical evidence for many language tools remains weak.

## Assumptions challenged

Detection is not resolution; trace completeness is not decision quality; natural
language flexibility is not pure defect.

## Discriminating experiment

Compare a lightweight requirements package with the proposed profile on
continuation correctness, negotiation quality, update cost, and error discovery.

## Confidence and limits

High overlap confidence; Medium confidence transient state needs more.
