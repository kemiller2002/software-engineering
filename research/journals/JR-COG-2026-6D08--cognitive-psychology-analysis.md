# Cognitive Psychology Analysis

## Model and evidence

Working memory is limited; long-term schemas change effective complexity.
Interruption requires goal-memory reconstruction. Guidance effects depend on
expertise and can reverse [EV-COG-2026-27F1]. Disruption varies with context,
timing, source, and priority [EV-COG-2026-C5A4]. External cues can improve
resumption [EV-MAINT-2026-0A52].

## Counterevidence and interpretations

“Cognitive load” is often used as an unfalsifiable design slogan. Self-reported
load may not track performance. Redundancy can be harmful for experts but
protective in safety contexts. Lower effort can reduce learning.

## Reusable methods and outcomes

Dual-task measures, recall, delayed transfer, resumption lag, error rate,
calibration, eye tracking where justified, expertise stratification, and
retention tests.

## Coverage, gap, and challenged assumptions

Psychology explains recipient adaptation, cueing, memory limits, and calibration.
It rejects one-template-fits-all and “less effort is always better.” It does not
specify organizational authority or software lifecycle traceability.

## Discriminating experiment

Factor handoff detail by measured task expertise and risk. Expertise-by-detail
interaction should appear; absence would weaken adaptive-profile claims.

## Confidence and limits

High confidence in general mechanisms; Medium transfer to real software handoffs.
